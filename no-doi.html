ARK Policy

GateBreakOpen does not assign DOI identifiers. GateBreakOpen is designed to use ARK identifiers as its persistent identification system for online publications. This choice is coherent with GateBreakOpen's independent, zero-cost, flexible, and multi-format publishing model.

What is a DOI?

A DOI, or Digital Object Identifier, is a persistent digital identifier used to identify an object such as a journal article, book chapter, dataset, report, or other scholarly or professional item. A DOI usually has a structure such as 10.xxxx/xxxxx and is commonly resolved through doi.org.

DOI is widely used in scholarly publishing and is managed through a structured registration-agency ecosystem. It is often associated with formal academic publishing infrastructures, metadata registration, and publisher-based identifier workflows.

What is an ARK?

An ARK, or Archival Resource Key, is a persistent identifier designed to identify digital, physical, or abstract objects. ARK identifiers are open, flexible, decentralized, and suitable for long-term access to resources. An ARK can identify articles, books, chapters, datasets, archives, cultural objects, educational materials, and other types of resources.

An ARK usually includes a Name Assigning Authority Number, called NAAN, which identifies the organization or authority assigning the ARK. A GateBreakOpen ARK may follow a simple chronological structure, such as: ark:/NAAN/gatebreakopen.article.2026.0001.

GateBreakOpen choice GateBreakOpen does not assign DOI identifiers. GateBreakOpen assigns ARK identifiers to eligible publications.

Why GateBreakOpen Uses ARK Instead of DOI

Independent Identification

ARK is coherent with GateBreakOpen because it allows an independent platform to identify publications without presenting itself as a traditional peer-reviewed journal or as a DOI-based academic publisher.

Flexibility for Many Formats

GateBreakOpen accepts different contribution types, including articles, reviews, monographs, books, book chapters, notes, essays, open letters, conceptual papers, methodological papers, and thesis/dissertation-based contributions. ARK is flexible enough to support all these formats with a clear internal structure.

Chronological and Transparent Numbering

GateBreakOpen may assign ARKs in chronological order. For example: gatebreakopen.article.2026.0001, gatebreakopen.article.2026.0002, gatebreakopen.book.2026.0001. The same identifier must never be reused for a different object.

Clear Difference from Peer-Reviewed Journals

Using ARK helps GateBreakOpen maintain a clear identity: independent, zero-cost, online-only, not peer-reviewed, and based on formal QA rather than peer review. The identifier provides stable reference without implying DOI-based journal status, scientific validation, or academic certification.

Persistence Commitment

GateBreakOpen will use ARK identifiers responsibly by keeping each identifier stable, avoiding reuse, maintaining clear publication pages, and updating links when necessary. Persistence depends on responsible maintenance, not only on the identifier string.
