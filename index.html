GBO Project

GateBreakOpen is an open, independent, zero-cost, online-only, AI-assisted publishing space designed for intellectual content, secondary research, literature reviews, conceptual contributions, methodological reflections, essays, pre-prints, commentaries, notes, open letters, and other non-interventional works.

GateBreakOpen is not a peer-reviewed journal. It does not perform anonymous peer review, does not certify the validity of published content, and does not replace academic journals, scholarly publishers, universities, ethics committees, or institutional systems of validation.

Core idea

GateBreakOpen offers a public, accessible, and transparent space where authors can make their work available while fully accepting responsibility for it.

How the project started

GateBreakOpen was conceived as a response to a practical problem: many authors, independent researchers, professionals, students, practitioners, and non-affiliated contributors may have serious reflections, literature-based analyses, methodological ideas, review-style manuscripts, conceptual contributions, or pre-print materials, but do not always have access to conventional publishing channels. Traditional publication systems may involve costs, long timelines, institutional filters, publication charges, formatting barriers, and gatekeeping mechanisms that can discourage dissemination, especially for authors outside formal academic structures.

The project was designed as a digital publishing circuit where access does not depend on academic position, institutional prestige, financial capacity, or affiliation. GateBreakOpen does not replace academic journals, peer review, scholarly publishers, or official systems of validation. Instead, it offers a complementary route for online dissemination based on author responsibility, transparency, basic editorial checks, and clear limits.

Why GateBreakOpen exists

GateBreakOpen exists to give space to secondary research and non-interventional scholarly contributions, including literature reviews, systematic reviews, scoping reviews, umbrella reviews, methodological papers, conceptual papers, commentaries, essays, pre-prints, evidence summaries, and other work based on already existing information. The platform does not accept primary research involving direct testing, experimentation, intervention, observation, or new data collection on humans or animals.

This choice keeps the platform focused, safer, and clearer in its responsibility structure. By accepting only secondary research and non-interventional contributions, GateBreakOpen avoids the ethical, clinical, experimental, and regulatory complexity of human-subject or animal-subject research, while still allowing authors to publish analyses, syntheses, interpretations, and reflections based on existing sources.

The author is the owner and manager of the work

GateBreakOpen considers the author the owner, the responsible subject, and the manager of their own work.

An author is not simply a person who writes. An author conceives an idea, organizes it, defines its structure, selects sources, verifies references, decides the final form, manages the public presentation of the work, and accepts responsibility for its consequences.

In this view, the author is not a scribe. The author is the owner and manager of the work.

GateBreakOpen does not appropriate authors’ content, does not claim exclusivity over published works, and does not transform publication into a loss of control over the work. The author remains the holder of the content, the intellectual identity, and the public responsibility connected to what they decide to publish.

Publishing on GateBreakOpen means making a work visible and accessible. It does not mean transferring ownership, authorship, responsibility, or intellectual control to the platform.

AI and chatbots as tools of the new digital author

GateBreakOpen openly supports the responsible use of artificial intelligence, chatbots, and digital workflows as tools that assist the author.

AI can support the author in brainstorming, preliminary idea generation, organization of concepts, summaries, synthesis of materials, identification of interconnections between topics, suggestions, linguistic revision, translation, text structuring, formatting, section organization, reference checking, metadata preparation, clarity of presentation, and technical management of content.

For GateBreakOpen, the use of AI is not automatically a threat to authorship. It is a tool. Like the pen, the printing press, the typewriter, the computer, databases, statistical software, and reference managers, AI belongs to the long history of tools that accompany intellectual production.

GateBreakOpen rejects the idea of the author as a “pen fundamentalist”: not in the narrow sense of manual writing, but in the broader sense of rigid and dogmatic dependence on only the methodologies currently accepted, only the tools currently recognized, and only the editorial rituals considered legitimate at a specific historical moment.

Every era has its dominant tools and its emerging tools. What today appears new, suspicious, or not fully accepted may tomorrow become an ordinary part of intellectual work. GateBreakOpen does not treat technical innovation as a fault. It treats it as a responsibility to be governed.

The value of a work does not depend on blind adherence to traditional tools, but on the responsibility with which the author thinks, verifies, organizes, declares, and presents the work.

AI does not replace the author. AI does not own the idea. AI does not remove human judgment. AI does not erase responsibility. The author remains the subject who directs the work, verifies the content, checks the sources, approves the final version, and publicly accepts responsibility for what is published.

In this sense, GateBreakOpen proposes a new paradigm of the digital author: not merely a writer limited by the boundaries of already approved tools, but a creator, curator, and manager of their own work, capable of using digital tools and AI-assisted workflows in a responsible, transparent, and conscious way.

Responsibility, rigor, and protection from pseudoscience

GateBreakOpen supports freedom of publication, but does not confuse freedom with absence of responsibility.

The platform does not accept pseudoscientific content, fringe science presented as established knowledge, conspiracy theories, data falsification, source manipulation, plagiarism, invented references, false DOI or PMID identifiers, nonexistent citations, unauthorized images, misleading content, misrepresentation of results, unsupported clinical claims, unjustified alarmism, or unvalidated therapeutic theories that may encourage dangerous behavior.

GateBreakOpen is not peer-reviewed, but this does not mean that it is a space without criteria. Publication is not automatic. Open access does not mean the right to publish anything.

Every contribution must meet minimum requirements of transparency, verifiability, author responsibility, source traceability, methodological clarity, and compatibility with the platform policies.

GateBreakOpen may refuse, suspend, request revision, restrict, or remove content that appears misleading, unverifiable, pseudoscientific, conspiratorial, unsafe, defamatory, discriminatory, manipulative, unlawful, or incompatible with the platform model.

This is not censorship. It is protection of the platform, authors, readers, and the credibility of the project.

In favor of open access, responsibility, and intellectual freedom

GateBreakOpen is also based on a clear cultural position: intellectual visibility should be able to depend on author responsibility, clarity of content, traceability of sources, transparency of declarations, and accessibility of the work, not only on economic barriers, institutional belonging, closed hierarchies, inherited editorial rituals, academic patronage networks, citation clubs, coercive citations, mandatory nomination of suggested reviewers, invisible networks of favor, or authorization systems that decide who may be seen and who must remain unseen.

GateBreakOpen supports knowledge that can circulate without payment barriers.

It supports intellectual visibility that does not depend exclusively on the name of the institution to which the author belongs.

It supports content that can become public without necessarily passing through a closed chain of authorization.

It supports submission procedures that do not require authors to indicate suggested reviewers, privileged contacts, internal figures, or strategic names as an explicit or implicit condition for entering editorial consideration.

It supports a correct relationship with the literature, based on necessary, relevant, and justified citations, not on citations imposed to increase metrics, prestige, or advantage for groups, journals, publishers, institutions, or networks of power.

It supports free and responsible writing, without forcing authors to align their thinking with the cultural, ideological, or editorial line of the container that hosts the work.

GateBreakOpen supports dialogue with the literature, but not citation as an obligatory tribute. It supports critical discussion, but not forced alignment. It supports method, but not editorial obedience. It supports the value of content, but not compulsory belonging. It supports rigor, but not barriers that transform rigor into a privilege of access. It supports quality, but not the monopoly of legitimation. It supports editorial transparency, but not the strategic nomination of reviewers as an entry filter or ritual of belonging. It supports the author’s contribution, but not the need to know, name, or please internal figures in order to be considered.

GateBreakOpen does not sell prestige. It does not promise academic recognition. It does not simulate peer review. It does not offer shortcuts to institutional validation.

Instead, it offers an open, declared, and responsible space where authors can publish non-interventional content, secondary research, reflections, reviews, analyses, and conceptual contributions without having to buy access, request belonging, undergo coercive citations, indicate suggested reviewers as a condition of entry, adapt to the thought of the editorial container, or depend on a closed chain of legitimation.

The principle is simple: the author must be able to publish, but must also be responsible. The platform must be open, but not naive. Access must be free, but not without rules.

GateBreakOpen in summary

GateBreakOpen is an open, independent, zero-cost, online-only, AI-assisted, and non-peer-reviewed space.

It recognizes the author as the owner, responsible subject, and manager of their own work.

It supports the responsible use of AI and chatbots as tools of the new digital author, useful for brainstorming, synthesis, interconnections, suggestions, revision, organization, and content management.

It rejects pseudoscience, fringe science, falsification, plagiarism, misrepresentation, unsafe content, manipulation, and deceptive use of sources.

It supports open access, author responsibility, public traceability, intellectual freedom, relevant citations, critical discussion, transparent submission procedures, and visibility not subordinated to mandatory nomination of suggested reviewers, coercive citations, or forced editorial alignment.

GateBreakOpen is not a space where everything counts. It is a space where the author can be free and responsible.
