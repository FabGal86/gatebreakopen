Type of Articles

GateBreakOpen accepts secondary research, literature-based work, conceptual work, methodological work, educational work, professional reflection, and non-interventional scholarly contributions. GateBreakOpen does not accept primary research involving direct testing, experimentation, intervention, observation, or new data collection on humans or animals.

Type of Articles

Degree-based and long-form works

Theses and Dissertations — B.Sc., M.Sc., and Ph.D.

GateBreakOpen accepts thesis- and dissertation-based works, including Bachelor of Science, Master of Science, and Ph.D. dissertations. These works are considered suitable for GateBreakOpen because they represent original personal scholarly contributions that have already been supervised, evaluated, and pre-approved by an academic institution. They may be adapted for publication as secondary, conceptual, theoretical, literature-based, methodological, educational, reflective, or non-interventional scholarly works. The author remains responsible for originality, institutional permissions, copyright, confidentiality, and compliance with GateBreakOpen policy.

Monographs

Long-form scholarly works focused on one specific topic, theory, method, author, field, problem, or body of literature. Monographs may be accepted when they are literature-based, conceptual, educational, historical, methodological, or theoretical and do not include prohibited primary human or animal research.

Books

Extended works organized into chapters or sections. GateBreakOpen may accept books that are scholarly, educational, conceptual, methodological, reflective, or literature-based and that comply with the platform's scope, policy, copyright requirements, and no-primary-research rule.

Book Chapters

Individual chapters that may be published as standalone contributions or as part of a larger book-style project. Chapters may include reviews, conceptual analyses, methodological explanations, educational content, theoretical discussion, historical analysis, or professional reflection.

Type of Articles

Literature and evidence review formats

Narrative Literature Reviews

Broad literature-based articles that describe, interpret, and discuss existing knowledge on a topic without necessarily following a rigid systematic protocol.

Traditional Literature Reviews

Reviews that summarize and organize existing publications, theories, evidence, and debates in a field or subfield.

Systematic Reviews

Structured reviews based on explicit questions, reproducible search strategies, selection criteria, screening procedures, and transparent synthesis of published evidence.

Scoping Reviews

Reviews that map the extent, range, and nature of literature on a topic, identify concepts, clarify definitions, and highlight knowledge gaps.

Umbrella Reviews

Reviews of reviews that summarize evidence from multiple systematic reviews, meta-analyses, or evidence syntheses on related questions.

Rapid Reviews

Accelerated evidence reviews using simplified or shortened review methods while maintaining transparency about methodological limitations.

Integrative Reviews

Reviews that combine evidence from different types of literature, methods, theories, or perspectives to provide a broader understanding of a topic.

Critical Reviews

Articles that evaluate, question, compare, or challenge existing literature, assumptions, theories, models, or practices.

Mapping Reviews

Reviews designed to classify and organize existing literature by topic, method, population, context, outcome, or research area.

Realist Reviews

Literature-based reviews that examine how, why, for whom, and under what conditions an intervention, phenomenon, or mechanism may work, using already published sources.

Meta-Reviews

Reviews that examine and compare multiple reviews, review methods, or bodies of synthesized evidence.

Methodological Reviews

Articles that analyze, compare, or explain research methods, analytical approaches, procedures, tools, or methodological debates.

Theoretical Reviews

Reviews focused on theories, conceptual frameworks, models, assumptions, and intellectual traditions in a specific field.

Conceptual Reviews

Articles that clarify, reorganize, redefine, or compare concepts and conceptual structures using existing literature.

Evidence Reviews

Articles that summarize and interpret the available evidence on a topic, question, practice, policy, or phenomenon.

State-of-the-Art Reviews

Reviews that present the current level of knowledge, major developments, recent trends, unresolved issues, and future directions in a field.

Type of Articles

Bibliometric, scientometric, meta-analytic, and secondary-data works

Bibliometric Reviews

Articles that analyze publication patterns, authorship, journals, keywords, citations, countries, institutions, and research trends using bibliographic data.

Scientometric Analyses

Studies of scientific production, impact, collaboration networks, research evolution, and knowledge structures using published metadata and citation data.

Citation Analyses

Articles that examine citation patterns, influential works, citation networks, and the intellectual influence of publications or authors.

Meta-Analyses Based on Published Aggregate Data

Quantitative syntheses that use already published aggregate results only, without collecting new human or animal data.

Secondary Analyses of Public, Anonymized, Non-Sensitive Datasets

Analyses based only on legally accessible, public, anonymized, non-sensitive datasets, where use is ethically and legally permitted.

Type of Articles

Theoretical, conceptual, philosophical, and taxonomy papers

Theoretical Articles

Articles that develop, compare, refine, or critique theories using reasoning and existing literature.

Conceptual Papers

Articles that propose new concepts, definitions, models, classifications, frameworks, or interpretive approaches.

New Theoretical/Conceptual Proposals

Original theoretical or conceptual proposals that introduce, refine, or organize new ideas, models, frameworks, classifications, definitions, interpretive structures, or scholarly perspectives without presenting primary human or animal research.

Philosophical Analyses

Articles that examine concepts, arguments, ethical issues, epistemological questions, or theoretical foundations from a philosophical perspective.

Taxonomy Papers

Articles that classify, organize, define, or structure concepts, practices, methods, tools, categories, problems, fields, or phenomena. Taxonomy Papers are especially suitable for conceptual, methodological, educational, and interdisciplinary contributions.

Type of Articles

Methodological, technical, workflow, tool, and checklist papers

Methodological Papers

Articles that present, explain, compare, or refine methods, procedures, tools, workflows, or analytical strategies.

Technical Notes

Focused articles that explain a technical procedure, tool, workflow, software approach, method, or applied process.

Applied Framework Proposals

Articles proposing practical frameworks, models, matrices, checklists, decision structures, conceptual maps, or applied tools for professional, educational, methodological, technological, organizational, or scholarly use.

Operational Model Papers

Articles presenting operational models, workflows, procedures, decision pathways, process structures, or implementation logic. These papers may be professional, educational, technical, or methodological, provided that they remain non-interventional and do not report prohibited primary research.

Professional Method Papers

Papers that describe, explain, organize, or refine professional methods, applied procedures, practical workflows, decision approaches, or field-based techniques. They may be used in marketing, management, education, healthcare, technology, communication, and other professional areas.

Technical Workflow Notes

Notes describing technical workflows, software procedures, automation pipelines, digital tools, data-processing steps, CRM workflows, AI-assisted processes, or operational sequences in a concise and educational format.

AI-Assisted Professional Workflows

Articles describing professional workflows supported by artificial intelligence, chatbots, automation tools, generative systems, or AI-assisted organization, writing, analysis, synthesis, or content-management processes. These contributions must clearly distinguish human responsibility from tool assistance.

Tool or Software Descriptions

Descriptive or educational articles presenting a tool, software, application, digital workflow, computational process, or technical system. They must not report user testing, human experimentation, or newly collected user data.

Checklist Articles

Articles proposing or explaining checklists for professional, educational, methodological, technical, organizational, or scholarly use. Checklists may support practice, learning, decision-making, quality control, or workflow organization.

Type of Articles

Viewpoint, commentary, essay, editorial, letter, and position formats

POV — Point of View Articles

Structured point-of-view articles presenting a clear personal, professional, scholarly, methodological, or cultural position. POV articles must remain lawful, non-denigrating, non-proselytizing, non-inciting, evidence-aware, and compatible with GateBreakOpen policy.

Open Letters

Public letters addressed to a community, institution, field, audience, or professional group. Open letters may be accepted when they are respectful, lawful, non-defamatory, non-discriminatory, non-threatening, non-proselytizing, and consistent with GateBreakOpen's prohibited-content rules.

Notes

Short scholarly, technical, educational, methodological, conceptual, or reflective contributions. Notes may present clarifications, observations, definitions, brief arguments, teaching points, technical explanations, or concise literature-based insights.

Opinion Articles

Reasoned articles presenting the author's perspective on a scholarly, professional, social, educational, methodological, or cultural issue.

Perspective Articles

Articles that offer a structured viewpoint, future direction, interpretive angle, or reflective analysis on an existing topic.

Commentaries

Short or medium-length articles discussing a topic, publication, debate, policy, method, problem, or current issue.

Essays

Argumentative, reflective, theoretical, cultural, or critical texts that develop an idea in a structured and scholarly way.

Editorial-Style Contributions

Short articles presenting an interpretive, programmatic, introductory, or reflective position on a topic or collection.

Position Papers

Articles that present and defend a clear scholarly, professional, methodological, educational, ethical, cultural, or policy position. They should be reasoned, respectful, evidence-aware, and consistent with GateBreakOpen's prohibited-content rules.

Professional Viewpoints

Structured viewpoints from practitioners, professionals, educators, consultants, managers, developers, healthcare workers, researchers, or field experts. Professional Viewpoints present reasoned perspectives without reporting prohibited primary research.

Type of Articles

Educational, teaching, glossary, and evidence-summary formats

Educational Articles

Articles designed to explain concepts, methods, tools, frameworks, or topics for learning, teaching, or professional development.

Evidence Summaries

Concise summaries of existing evidence, literature, reports, or guidelines on a specific question or topic.

Glossary Articles

Educational or methodological articles that define and explain key terms, concepts, acronyms, categories, or expressions used in a field, discipline, professional area, or technical domain.

Teaching Notes

Educational notes designed to explain a concept, case, method, framework, tool, workflow, or topic for students, professionals, educators, or general scholarly readers.

Type of Articles

Policy, guideline, book, article, and historical analysis formats

Policy Analyses

Articles that examine policies, regulations, guidelines, institutional decisions, social measures, or governance issues using existing information.

Book Reviews

Critical or descriptive analyses of books, monographs, manuals, textbooks, or other published volumes.

Article Reviews

Critical or descriptive analyses of one or more published articles, including their arguments, methods, relevance, and limitations.

Historical Analyses

Articles that examine events, developments, authors, movements, institutions, or ideas from a historical perspective.

Guideline Commentaries

Commentaries on existing guidelines, recommendations, standards, protocols, policies, or professional documents. They may discuss clarity, limitations, applicability, implications, or alignment with existing literature.

Type of Articles

Professional reflection, case commentary, practice, and lessons learned

Professional Reflections

Reflective articles based on professional experience, practice, interpretation, or lessons learned, without reporting prohibited primary research.

Professional Case Commentary

Structured professional commentaries based on a practical case, work-related situation, professional decision, strategy, problem, solution, or lesson learned. Professional Case Commentaries must remain non-interventional, avoid identifiable personal or confidential business data, and must not report primary human or animal research.

Professional Case Reflection

Reflective articles based on professional experience with a specific case, situation, project, or practice. These contributions focus on interpretation, lessons learned, practical implications, and professional reasoning without presenting experimental, observational, or newly collected human or animal data.

Practice-Based Commentary

Commentaries grounded in professional practice, field experience, applied reasoning, or work-based expertise. They may discuss practical problems, decisions, methods, and implications, provided that they do not involve prohibited primary research, intervention, observation, or new data collection on humans or animals.

Professional Experience Reports

Structured reports based on professional experience, work processes, applied practice, or lessons learned from real-world activity. They must be reflective, educational, methodological, or conceptual and must not disclose confidential, sensitive, or identifiable information.

Field Practice Reflections

Reflective contributions based on practice in professional, organizational, educational, technical, or field contexts. Field Practice Reflections focus on interpretation and learning rather than systematic observation, direct testing, intervention, or new data collection.

Lessons Learned Articles

Articles that extract practical, conceptual, educational, methodological, or professional lessons from a project, experience, case, process, strategy, or challenge. They should present transferable insights without reporting prohibited primary research or confidential information.

Practice Notes

Short practical contributions presenting a focused professional insight, technical clarification, operational solution, applied method, teaching point, or concise reflection from practice.

Type of Articles

Marketing, communication, business, and industry formats

Applied Marketing Case Analysis

Non-interventional analyses of marketing cases, strategies, funnels, campaigns, branding decisions, communication processes, customer journey models, or sales approaches. These articles must be descriptive, reflective, conceptual, or methodological and must not include identifiable customer data or experimental marketing research.

Marketing Practice Report

Professional reports describing marketing practices, digital strategies, communication workflows, positioning approaches, campaign structures, lead-generation methods, or sales-related processes. Reports must avoid confidential business information, identifiable personal data, and prohibited primary research.

Business Case Commentary

Commentaries on business, management, organizational, sales, operational, entrepreneurial, or strategic cases. These contributions may present practical interpretation and lessons learned while remaining non-interventional and compliant with confidentiality, copyright, and ethical requirements.

Digital Marketing Commentaries

Commentaries focused on digital marketing topics such as SEO, social media communication, online advertising, content marketing, email marketing, automation, CRM, lead generation, digital funnels, and platform-based communication strategies.

Strategic Communication Commentaries

Articles discussing communication strategy, branding, positioning, messaging, institutional communication, commercial communication, audience engagement, media choices, or public-facing professional communication.

Industry Commentaries

Commentaries analyzing a professional sector, market, industry, field, trend, organizational context, or applied domain. Industry Commentaries may discuss developments, challenges, practices, and future directions using non-interventional and lawful sources.

Type of Articles

Preprints, protocols, working papers, white papers, and brief reports

Research Protocols Without Active Data Collection

Protocols or planned review papers that do not involve recruitment, testing, intervention, experimentation, or direct human or animal data collection at the time of publication.

Preprints

Preliminary versions of scholarly, professional, conceptual, methodological, educational, reflective, or literature-based works shared before or outside formal journal publication. Authors remain responsible for originality, permissions, copyright, and compliance with GateBreakOpen policy.

Working Papers

Preliminary or developing papers that present ideas, models, methods, arguments, conceptual structures, literature-based analyses, or professional reflections before a final or expanded version is prepared.

White Papers

Structured documents that present a problem, context, solution, proposal, model, position, or technical-professional explanation. White Papers may be accepted when they are lawful, non-promotional in a deceptive sense, non-interventional, and compatible with GateBreakOpen policy.

Brief Reports Without Primary Data

Short reports that summarize a topic, problem, model, practice, method, source, document, guideline, or secondary dataset without recruiting participants, testing subjects, performing interventions, or collecting new human or animal data.
